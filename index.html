04 — Culture

A living heritage.

Not artefacts behind glass — hanbok are rented for palace walks, hanok are inhabited, and customs are practised every day.

한복

Hanbok 한복

Traditional dress with curved lines and bright colours — worn for Seollal, weddings and palace photo sessions.

한옥

Hanok & Heritage 한옥

Timber houses with diamond roofs and the ancient ondol heating system — Bukchon and Jeonju are its living galleries.

예

Etiquette & Respect 예의

Bowing when you meet, two hands when giving and receiving — the Jongmyo jeryeak ceremony is still held every year.

05 — Traditions

A calendar full of celebrations.

From New Year rice-cake soup to glowing lanterns across the river — pick a date and Korea will celebrate it with you.

Seollal 설날

Lunar New Year: families go home, wear hanbok, honour their elders and eat tteokguk — the rice-cake soup believed to add a year to your age.

Chuseok 추석

"Harvest thanksgiving": songpyeon are made together, charye rites are held, and the brightest full moon of the year rises.

Honouring Tradition 전통

Jongmyo ritual music, talchum mask dance and the Gangneung Danoje earth festival — several are UNESCO-recognised.

Celebration Timeline
Seollal · 설날

The first day of the lunar calendar — children eat a bowl of tteokguk and symbolically "add a year", while the whole country slows down for the busiest homecoming journey of the year.

Yeondeunghoe · 연등회

On Buddha's Birthday in early May, tens of thousands of paper lanterns light up Jongno in Seoul — listed as UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage since 2020.

Danoje · 단오제

Korea's oldest earth festival in Gangneung — shamanic rites, mask dances and traditional games over 20 days, UNESCO-recognised.

Chuseok · 추석

On the eighth month of the lunar calendar, families gather for charye, visit ancestral graves and share songpyeon under the full moon.

Jinju Yudeung · 진주 남강 유등축제

For two weeks every October, thousands of lanterns light the Nam River in Jinju — remembering the defence of Jinjuseong fortress in 1592.
